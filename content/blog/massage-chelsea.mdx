Chelsea has always taken looking after itself seriously. Between the King's Road, Sloane Square and the quiet streets running down to the Embankment, SW3 and SW10 have more spas, studios and clinics per street than almost anywhere in London.

That is good news if you want a massage — and slightly overwhelming if you want a *good* one. Here is how to make sense of it.

## What Chelsea Offers

### The spa experience

Most of what you will find on and around the King's Road is the day-spa model: beautiful interiors, a long treatment menu, and massage offered alongside facials and beauty treatments. It is reliable and pleasant, and it is priced to match the postcode.

What it rarely is, is personal. Appointments run to the clock, the next client is waiting, and the therapist you liked may not be there next time.

### Independent therapists

The alternative is an independent therapist working from her own studio. You deal with one person from the first message to the last minute of the session. The pace is set by you, not by a booking system, and the price reflects the massage rather than the rent on a shop front.

For anyone who wants to come back regularly — or who wants a session with real attention in it — it is usually the better choice.

## What Chelsea Clients Tend to Want

Looking after clients from this part of London, I have noticed a pattern. People from Chelsea rarely ask for "a massage". They ask for **time**: an hour that is completely theirs, with nobody hurrying them and no phone in reach.

They also care a great deal about **discretion**. That is one reason a private studio suits them better than a busy reception area on a high street.
