Other therapists ask me this surprisingly often — usually by message, sometimes in person: *where do you advertise, and is it worth it?* After several years working independently in London and trying most of the big platforms, my honest answer has become very simple: [MassageHub](https://massagehub.uk) is where my profile lives, and it is the only platform I renew every month without thinking twice.

This is my personal experience — not a sponsored post. If you are a therapist weighing up where to put your advertising budget, or simply curious how this side of the industry works, here is my honest breakdown.

## What I Was Looking For

When you work for yourself, your advertising has to do three jobs: bring enough enquiries, bring the *right* enquiries, and not eat half your income in the process. That sounds obvious, but very few platforms manage all three.

I have tried general classifieds sites, social media, and massage directories. Over time, one lesson became crystal clear: **where you advertise shapes the clients you attract.** The platform is a filter, whether you like it or not.

## Why MassageHub Won Me Over

### A steady flow of genuine clients

The first thing that struck me after publishing my profile was the volume of enquiries — and, more importantly, the ratio of enquiries to actual bookings. People browsing a dedicated massage directory are looking for exactly one thing: a massage. They have usually read my profile, seen my [services](/services) and prices, and message me ready to book a time.

That sounds like a small thing. It is not. It is the difference between spending your morning confirming appointments and spending it answering questions that go nowhere.

### An interface that respects your time

Publishing and updating a profile on MassageHub is genuinely straightforward — photos, description, services, working hours, all edited in a few clicks. When I change my availability or update pictures, it takes minutes.

If you have ever fought with a clunky classifieds dashboard that logs you out, buries your ad, or makes you re-enter everything to change one line, you will understand why I mention this at all. Your listing is your shop window; being able to keep it fresh without friction matters.

### Honest, predictable pricing

At the time of writing, a standard listing is **£50 a month**, and a VIP upgrade is **another £50** on top. That is the entire pricing model. No credit systems, no pay-per-bump mechanics designed to drain your balance, no surprise fees.

For a working therapist, predictability is worth almost as much as the price itself. I know exactly what my advertising costs each month, and one or two bookings cover it. Everything after that is return.

### Clients who read, respect and show up

Here is the part that genuinely changed my working week: the *quality* of clients. The people who contact me through MassageHub are, on the whole, polite, punctual and respectful of my boundaries and prices. They book the session I actually offer — whether that is a [sensual massage](/services/sensual-massage) or a longer [tantric session](/services/tantric-massage) — and they arrive on time.

Every independent therapist knows this is not something to take for granted.

## Where Other Platforms Fell Short for Me

I want to be fair here: platforms like **Vivastreet** and **AdultWork** are huge, and plenty of people use them. But my personal experience advertising massage services on them was draining, and I know from conversations with other therapists that I am far from alone.

The pattern, in my experience, was always the same:

- **Endless discount hunting.** A significant share of messages opened with a request for a lower price — before asking a single question about the massage itself.
- **Requests for "extras" I do not offer.** My profile is clear about what I do. On the big classifieds sites, many people simply did not read it — or did not care.
- **Time wasters.** Long back-and-forth conversations, confirmed bookings that never showed up, and evenings held free for clients who vanished. For someone whose income is their diary, no-shows are not an annoyance; they are lost rent.

None of this means those platforms are "bad" — they are simply generalist marketplaces with enormous, mixed audiences. But when your work depends on trust, boundaries and a calm atmosphere, a filter that fails to filter costs you far more than the listing fee.

## The Difference a Dedicated Directory Makes

The conclusion I have landed on, after years of trial and error: **specialist platforms attract serious clients.** Someone who has navigated to a UK massage directory, chosen their city, and read through profiles is a fundamentally different visitor from someone scrolling a classifieds feed.

My MassageHub profile has quietly become the backbone of my practice — steady enquiries, respectful clients, and a fixed, fair cost I never think about. My [own website](/) and word of mouth do the rest.
