Central London has more places to get a massage than anywhere else in the country. Walk through Soho, Mayfair, Covent Garden or Fitzrovia and you will pass a spa, a clinic or a massage shop every few minutes.

That abundance is the problem. With so much choice and so little to tell one door from the next, how do you choose well?

## The Landscape

### Hotel spas and wellness centres

The West End's luxury hotels have some of the most beautiful spas in the city. The settings are extraordinary and the prices reflect it. Treatments are polished and consistent. If you want an occasion, this is it.

### Chains and booking apps

There are chain clinics across zones 1 and 2, and apps that will send a therapist to you. They are convenient and easy to book. The catch is that you are booking a time slot rather than a person — who you get can be different every time.

### High-street massage shops

Particularly around Soho and Chinatown, there are many walk-in shops. Some are very good; some are not. It is hard to know from the street, so look for clear prices, a clean interior and proper private rooms.

### Independent therapists

An independent therapist works for herself, from her own studio. You speak to her directly, she prepares the room for you, and the session is built around what you want. Because studio rents in W1 are so high, many independents work a few stops out of the centre.

## Why People Travel a Few Stops West

I hear the same thing from clients who work in the West End: it is difficult to relax two streets from the office. The noise, the crowds, the feeling that you could be called back at any moment — none of it helps.

A short journey changes that. Fifteen minutes on the Piccadilly line and you are in a quiet residential part of West London, with the day already behind you. The session starts before you arrive.
