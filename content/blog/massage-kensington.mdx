Kensington sits at the western edge of central London, with Kensington Gardens to the north and the museum quarter to the south. It is an area of garden squares, embassies, mansion blocks and grand hotels — and, unsurprisingly, one of the best-served parts of the city for massage.

But "best-served" and "best for you" are not the same thing. Here is an honest look at the options in and around Kensington, written by someone who works just next door in West Kensington.

## The Three Kinds of Massage in Kensington

### Hotel spas

Kensington's larger hotels have spas, and they are lovely places: immaculate rooms, premium products, robes and herbal tea. What you get is a polished, consistent, slightly standardised treatment. It is ideal for a special occasion or a gift.

The trade-offs are price — you are paying for the surroundings as much as the massage — and the fact that you will often see a different therapist each time.

### Wellness clinics

Around High Street Kensington and South Kensington you will find clinics offering massage alongside physiotherapy, osteopathy and acupuncture. These are clinically minded and well suited to a specific ache or injury. The atmosphere is closer to a consulting room than a retreat.

### Independent therapists

An independent therapist works for herself, usually from her own studio. You see the same person every time, the session is shaped around you rather than a treatment menu, and prices are generally lower than a hotel spa because there is no hotel to pay for.

This is how I work — which is why I am biased, and happy to say so.

## What to Look For

Whichever route you choose, a few things tell you a lot before you book:

- **Clear, published prices.** You should know the cost before you arrive.
- **A real conversation first.** A good therapist asks what you want from the session.
- **Unhurried time.** An hour should mean an hour.
- **A clean, warm, private room.** It matters more than any product line.
