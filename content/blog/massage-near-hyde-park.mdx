Hyde Park is the green centre of this side of London, and four very different neighbourhoods press up against its railings. Where you are standing when you search for "massage near Hyde Park" changes the answer quite a lot.

So here is the park, side by side.

## North: Bayswater and Lancaster Gate

The north side is hotel country. Bayswater Road, Lancaster Gate and the streets behind them are full of hotels, serviced apartments and visitors. Massage here means the occasional hotel spa and a long row of small massage shops around Queensway.

It is convenient and varied in quality. If you are staying on this side, it is worth knowing that a quieter option is only a few Tube stops away.

## South: Knightsbridge

The south side is the most expensive. Knightsbridge is home to famous department stores and some of London's grandest hotels, and its spas are spectacular — and priced that way. Come here for an experience; do not expect it to be an everyday habit.

## West: Kensington

Beyond Kensington Gardens, the park gives way to the embassies, garden squares and mansion blocks of Kensington. This side has hotel spas and wellness clinics, and a calmer, more residential feel. I have written more in my [guide to massage in Kensington](/blog/massage-kensington).

## East: Mayfair and Park Lane

Park Lane's hotels look straight onto the park, and Mayfair lies just behind them. Again: beautiful spas, high prices, and a very polished style of treatment.

## What's Missing Around the Park

Notice the pattern. Around Hyde Park you can easily find a hotel spa or a high-street shop. What is much harder to find is the thing in between: a private, personal, unhurried session with one therapist, in a room prepared just for you, at a sensible price.

That is the gap I work in.
